people are not fungible - rough draft

🚧 this page is a work in progress!

many aspects of our society tend to treat individuals as fungible and replaceable units, differentiated by only a small set of attributes.

employers view employees as replaceable units that can be easily exchanged for another employee with a similar skillset. "job security" is a thing of the past for most.

social media driven influencer culture has created a world where your reach is determined by your appearance and superficial aspects of your personality, and people become unhealthily obsessed with comparing themselves to the few people who rise to the top of their feeds.

dating apps only differentiate individuals by a few factors like height, weight, and appearance. this isn't an accident, it's a result of revealed preferences.

more and more aspects of interactions and relationships are becoming transactional, as we begin to view people more and more not as an end but a means to an end.

for example, the culturally agreed upon definition of "friendship" has slowly shrunk as this transactional attitude has permeated into every aspect of our lives.
helping out a friend going through a rough time isn't part of being a friend anymore, it's "emotional labor" that should be delegated to a therapist.
we don't commit to sticking by our friends when times get tough; social media has made it much easier to just find somebody else.

and then we wonder why everybody is so lonely and atomized and fucked up?
