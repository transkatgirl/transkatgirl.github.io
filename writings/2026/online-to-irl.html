being raised by the internet

when i was in school, i always stuck out like a sore thumb. i was autistic, nerdy, weird, and the other students could smell my still-in-the-closet queerness from a mile away. i was relentlessly bullied just for existing.

the only friends at school i could make were the "special needs" students. they were really sweet, but we usually had little in common, and being seen around them only intensified the bullying.

so, i sought out other options.

i made my first Twitter account when i was 13.

everyone loves to talk about how terrible Twitter is, and well, for the average Twitter user, i'm inclined to agree with them. if you aren't careful about how you use it, Twitter can be an absolute mental-health-ruining hellsite that can drive people to really dark places.

but, despite it all, Twitter changed my life for the better by introducing me to some of the best friends i've ever had.

friends who helped me discover
who i was,
what possibilities the world holds,
what i can become,
and who shaped me into the person i am today.

friends who were there for me when nobody else was,
who made the low points of my life tolerable,
who guided me towards better decisions,
and who saved my life far more times than i'd like to admit.

(i strongly believe that social media can be a force for good, and i'd like to write a blog post about this someday.)

long distances

when i was young, i promised myself that as soon as i could, i'd like to spend the rest of my life living with, or at least nearby, all of my online friends.

sadly, life is complicated and hard.

the internet is global. not all of my friends live in one city, one region, or even one country.

and, even though most of us want to move, and are relatively young, we still have things tying us to the places we live.
it could be a job, an obligation to take care of a family member, a sentimental attachment to the place, or just being unable to reach the financial escape velocity required for such a move.

and then, to top it all off, borders keep some of my friends from living in my country, and me from living in theirs.
