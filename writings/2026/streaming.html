my irl streaming setup

written 2026-03-10

streaming irl is challenging, even when considered purely from a technical standpoint. connection quality can vary wildly as you walk around and connections can suddenly drop without warning. weather conditions will change suddenly and your setup will be subjected to whatever mother nature throws at it. everything has to be battery powered and any additional weight makes the setup more exhausting to carry around.

and then there's the financial cost. unless you're already a successful streamer, you've likely got a tight budget to work with, and the cost of the necessary equipment adds up fast.

in the interest of helping out other people who are trying to do irl streaming, i decided to put together a list of everything in my (modest, but decent quality) irl streaming setup.

however, in the process of putting this together, there are tradeoffs i made which may not be acceptable for other people. i prioritized portability and reliability over video/audio quality, and weatherproofing+ergonomics over portability.

this is what what i keep in my weather-resistant backpack:

category item explanation or comments
recording equipment iPhone 15 Pro the center of the setup; it's simultaneously the camera, video encoder, and one of the cellular uplinks (any iPhone >= 12 will likely work fine in this role, but newer is better).
DJI Mic 3 (comes with Windscreen) a high-quality wireless mic with good battery life. however, it sadly requires a USB-C receiver for usable sound quality, and neither the receiver nor the unit itself is waterproof.
note: if you decide to get a different microphone, make sure to get a windscreen for it, as windscreens are required for reliably usable outdoor audio.
weatherproofing Gossamer Travel Umbrella an absolute necessity; most streaming gear isn't water-resistant, and you will eventually get caught off guard by rain when you least expect it.
(if you don't need an umbrella because all of your handheld gear is waterproof, you should get a backpack rainfly, as rain will slowly seep into all backpacks when given enough time.)
Gossamer Hands-free Umbrella Clamp you do not want to end up holding an umbrella for several hours, and this offers an easy way to mount an umbrella to a backpack.
i'd strongly recommend buying extras, as they're cheap and easy to lose.
camera mounting (WIP; trying to find better gear) Fantaseal Ergonomic Phone Handle essential if you're going to be holding the camera/phone for long periods of time. this specific handle is the most lightweight one i've found so far.
Telesin Magnetic Phone Neck Mount neck mounts are the least-bad POV livestreaming option. you can still see the phone's display for reading chat, and unlike head mounts, they're not horrendously uncomfortable to wear for extended periods (they still kind of suck to wear).
Telesin Magnetic Flexible Tripod useful for when you're going to be sitting somewhere for a while and need something to hold the phone up.
batteries and charging Anker 737 Power Bank affordable, high capacity, and supports up to 140W USB PD in/out. may be somewhat overkill for shorter streams, where a smaller phone-only power bank is fine.
Nestout USB-C Cable useful for charging equipment while outdoors; it supports 100W USB PD and is IP54 rated when the endcaps are on
i'd recommend buying extras; it's worth the additional cost to make sure you have a backup in case one is broken or lost.

NETGEAR Omnidirectional Cellular Antenna an external cellular antenna can significantly improve connection stability and available bandwidth when well placed, especially in areas with poor coverage. just make sure to get an omnidirectional antenna.

now we can get into the software side of things.

i use Moblin as my streaming app, as it's open source and has pretty much all of the features that most streamers would want.

assuming you're not streaming from somewhere with a stable, high-bandwidth internet connection, you will need to stream to a transcoding server and then have the transcoding server stream to your target streaming platforms. using a transcoding server offers the following advantages:

  • it allows you to use the SRT protocol, which is much better at handling unreliable connections and changes in available bandwidth
  • it allows you to bond together multiple connections using SRTLA to increase available bandwidth and improve reliability
  • it adds disconnect protection, so that the stream doesn't end when the connection drops
  • it allows you to stream to multiple platforms without a multiplicative increase in your bandwidth usage

(the disadvantage of using a transcoding server is increased latency, cost, and setup complexity.)

i use Streamrun as my transcoding server due to its ease of use, affordability, and reliability. however, if you already have the necessary equipment and are willing to spend some time tinkering, you can host a transcoding server yourself using mediamtx, ffmpeg and srtla.

when it comes to the fallback stream, i personally use a test pattern w/ silent audio (i personally really like GStreamer's test pattern, which is used by Streamrun Pro's test video) and overlaid text saying "Technical Difficulties", as generic "No Signal" or "Stream is Offline" messages can be confusing to viewers.


notes on self-hosting a transcoding server

i briefly played around with trying to run my own transcoding server before i decided it probably wasn't worth the hassle. however, in the interest of helping out others who would like to do so, here's the configuration i came up with: 

# [mediamtx defaults ommitted]
readTimeout: 1s # Should be set to SRT latency
# [mediamtx defaults ommitted]

paths:
    compressed:
        runOnReady: >
            ffmpeg -fflags +flush_packets+nobuffer -rtsp_transport tcp -i rtsp://localhost:$RTSP_PORT/$MTX_PATH -c copy -map 0:v -map 0:a -f tee -use_fifo true -fifo_options drop_pkts_on_overflow=1:attempt_recovery=1:recovery_wait_time=1 "[f=flv]rtmp://localhost:1935/example1|[f=flv]rtmp://localhost:1935/example2|[f=flv]rtmp://localhost:1935/example3"
        runOnReadyRestart: yes
    ingest:
        alwaysAvailable: true
        alwaysAvailableTracks:
            - codec: H265
            - codec: MPEG4Audio
              sampleRate: 48000
              channelCount: 1
        runOnReady: >
            ffmpeg -fflags +flush_packets+nobuffer -flags low_delay -rtsp_transport tcp -i rtsp://localhost:$RTSP_PORT/$MTX_PATH -af "aresample=flags=res:async=1,aformat=channel_layouts=stereo" -vf "fps=fps=30,format=yuv420p,scale=1280x720:flags=lanczos" -c:v libx264 -preset medium -tune zerolatency -profile:v high -g 60 -b:v 7000k -minrate 7000k -maxrate 7000k -bufsize 7000k -c:a libfdk_aac -b:a 160k -max_muxing_queue_size 1024 -f rtsp rtsp://localhost:$RTSP_PORT/compressed
        runOnReadyRestart: yes
you'll need a build of ffmpeg that includes the libfdk_aac encoder to run this (if you're on MacOS, you can replace the aac encoder -c:a [encoder] with aac_at). ffmpeg's built-in aac encoder really sucks and you should avoid it at all costs.

i have no idea how well this works in real-world conditions, as i never ended up using my DIY transcoding server to stream to popular streaming services. however, if you do end up trying it (or if you already have a configuration that works for you), please let me know so that i can update this post (contact info is on my blog's home page).

(if you can't get ffmpeg as a transcoder to work properly, you can always use OBS for that role instead. the main reason i wanted to avoid doing so was because OBS has much heavier resource usage than ffmpeg alone, making it far more costly to host on a cloud VPS.)

these are the settings i'd recommend using in Moblin:

  • Stream:
    • URL (with connection bonding): make sure it starts with srtla://
    • URL (without connection bonding): make sure it starts with srt://
    • SRT(LA):
      • Latency (WiFi): 4x streaming server RTT
      • Latency (Cellular): 1500ms to 3000ms (higher latency improves stream stability, lower allows for more interactive experiences)
        • If low latency is far more important than stream stability, use 1000ms
        • If stream stability is more important than low latency, use 3000ms
      • Latency (Moving Starlink): 5000ms
      • Adaptive bitrate: on
        • Algorithm: BELABOX
        • Minimum bitrate: 250 kbit/s
      • Big packets: on
      • Implementation (latency >= 1s): Moblin
      • Implementation (latency < 1s): Official
    • Video:
      • Resolution: 720p to 1080p
      • FPS: 30 (only use 60 if you're capturing activities with lots of motion)
      • Low light boost: on
      • Codec: H.265
      • Bitrate (1080p60): 15 mbit/s
      • Bitrate (1080p30): 10 mbit/s
      • Bitrate (720p60): 9 mbit/s
      • Bitrate (720p30): 6 mbit/s
      • Keyframe interval: 1s
      • B-frames: off
      • Adaptive resolution: on
        • Thresholds: Adjust upwards until <250 kbit/s = 160p
      • Timecodes: off (unless you need them)
    • Audio:
      • Codec: AAC (result in lower generation loss compared to Opus)
      • Bitrate (mono): 128 kbit/s
      • Bitrate (stereo): 256 kbit/s
      • Only use stereo if your content actually benefits from it (this usually isn't the case; dialogue sounds better in mono)
    • Background streaming: on
  • Camera:
    • Video stabilization: Standard
    • Fixed horizon: off
    • Mirror front camera on stream: on
    • Color space: Standard RGB (streaming in HDR isn't supported by most streaming platforms, and will require additional configuration not mentioned here)
  • Audio:
    • Mic: [Select your recording microphone]
    • Bluetooth output only: on
    • Prefer stereo mic: off
  • Debug:
    • Builtin audio and video delay: 0

(when it comes to the encoder settings on your streaming server, just use whatever your target streaming platforms recommend)

don't forget to:

  • configure your overlays, scenes, and widgets as you see fit
  • configure all of the streaming platforms you stream to within Moblin (lets you read and moderate your chat within the app)
  • test everything end-to-end (so, do a test stream to your streaming server using all of your equipment) before going live!
  • configure moblin's built-in browser so that you can do common tasks (reading notes, starting/stopping your streaming server, changing the stream title) without leaving the app
