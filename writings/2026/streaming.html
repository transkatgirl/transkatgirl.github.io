my irl streaming setup

written 2026-02-27

streaming irl is challenging, even when considered purely from a technical standpoint. connection quality can vary wildly as you walk around and connections can suddenly drop without warning. weather conditions will change suddenly and your setup will be subjected to whatever mother nature throws at it. everything has to be battery powered and any additional weight makes the setup more exhausting to carry around.

and then there's the financial cost. unless you're already a successful streamer, you've likely got a tight budget to work with, and the cost of the necessary equipment adds up fast.

in the interest of helping out other people who are trying to do irl streaming, i decided to put together a list of everything in my (modest, but decent quality) irl streaming setup.

however, in the process of putting this together, there are tradeoffs i made which may not be acceptable for other people. i prioritized portability and reliability over video/audio quality, and weatherproofing+ergonomics over portability.

this is what what i keep in my weather-resistant backpack:

category item explanation or comments
recording equipment iPhone 15 Pro the center of the setup; it's simultaneously the camera, video encoder, and one of the cellular uplinks (any iPhone >= 12 will likely work fine in this role, but newer is better).
DJI Mic 3 (comes with Windscreen) a high-quality wireless mic with good battery life. however, it sadly requires a USB-C receiver for usable sound quality, and neither the receiver nor the unit itself is waterproof.

now we can get into the software side of things.

i use Moblin as my streaming app, as it's open source and has pretty much all of the features that most streamers would want.

i highly recommend that you use the SRT(LA) protocol for streaming: it's UDP-based, supports adaptively adjusting the bitrate based on network conditions, and SRTLA allows for bonding multiple connections together to increase available bandwidth and reliability.

in addition, you'll also want to make sure that your streaming endpoint supports some form of disconnect prevention, so that the stream doesn't end when the connection drops.

because none of the major streaming platforms support the SRT(LA) protocol, i use Streamrun as a transcoding server due to its ease of use, affordability, and reliability. however, if you already have the necessary equipment and are willing to spend some time tinkering, you can host a transcoding server yourself using mediamtx, ffmpeg and srtla.

when it comes to your fallback stream, i'd recommend using a test pattern w/ silent audio (i personally really like GStreamer's test pattern, which is used by Streamrun Pro's test video), as "No Signal" or "Stream is Offline" messages can be confusing to viewers.


notes on self-hosting a transcoding server

i briefly played around with trying to run my own transcoding server before i decided it probably wasn't worth the hassle. however, in the interest of helping out others who would like to do so, here's the configuration i came up with: 

# [mediamtx defaults ommitted]

paths:
    compressed:
        runOnReady: >
            ffmpeg -fflags nobuffer -rtsp_transport tcp -i rtsp://localhost:$RTSP_PORT/$MTX_PATH -c copy -f flv rtmp://localhost:1935/example1 -c copy -f flv rtmp://localhost:1935/example2 -c copy -f flv rtmp://localhost:1935/example3
        runOnReadyRestart: yes
    ingest:
        alwaysAvailable: true
        alwaysAvailableTracks:
            - codec: H265
            - codec: MPEG4Audio
              sampleRate: 48000
              channelCount: 1
        runOnReady: >
            ffmpeg -fflags nobuffer -flags low_delay -rtsp_transport tcp -i rtsp://localhost:$RTSP_PORT/$MTX_PATH -af "aresample=flags=res:async=1,aformat=channel_layouts=stereo" -vf "fps=fps=30,format=yuv420p,scale=1280x720:flags=lanczos" -c:v libx264 -preset medium -tune zerolatency -profile:v high -g 60 -b:v 7000k -minrate 7000k -maxrate 7000k -bufsize 7000k -c:a libfdk_aac -b:a 160k -max_muxing_queue_size 1024 -f rtsp rtsp://localhost:$RTSP_PORT/compressed
        runOnReadyRestart: yes
you'll need a build of ffmpeg that includes the libfdk_aac encoder to run this (if you're on MacOS, you can replace the aac encoder -c:a [encoder] with aac_at). ffmpeg's built-in aac encoder really sucks and you should avoid it at all costs.

i have no idea how well this works in real-world conditions, as i never ended up using this configuration to stream to popular streaming services. however, if you do end up trying it (or if you already have a configuration that works for you), please let me know so that i can update this post (contact info is on my blog's home page).

(if you can't get ffmpeg as a transcoder to work properly, you can always use OBS for that role instead. the main reason i wanted to avoid doing so was because OBS has much heavier resource usage than ffmpeg alone, making it far more costly to host on a cloud VPS.)

these are the settings i'd recommend using in Moblin:

  • Stream:
    • URL (with connection bonding): make sure it starts with srtla://
    • URL (without connection bonding): make sure it starts with srt://
    • SRT(LA):
      • Latency: 1500ms
        • increase in increments of 500ms (up to 3000ms) if you encounter dropped keyframes
        • if you're willing to accept dropped keyframes, can be decreased to 1000ms without affecting battery life
      • Adaptive bitrate: on
        • Algorithm: BELABOX
        • Minimum bitrate: 250 kbit/s
      • Big packets: on
      • Implementation: Moblin
    • Video:
      • Resolution: 720p to 1080p, depending on expected network conditions
      • FPS: 30 (only use 60 if you're capturing activities with lots of motion)
      • Low light boost: on
      • Codec: H.265
      • Bitrate (1080p60): 12 mbit/s
      • Bitrate (1080p30): 8 mbit/s
      • Bitrate (720p60): 7.5 mbit/s
      • Bitrate (720p30): 5 mbit/s
      • Keyframe interval: 2s
      • B-frames: off
      • Adaptive resolution: on
        • Thresholds: Adjust upwards until <250 kbit/s = 160p
      • Timecodes: off (unless you need them)
    • Audio:
      • Codec: AAC (result in lower generation loss compared to Opus)
      • Bitrate (mono): 128 kbit/s
      • Bitrate (stereo): 256 kbit/s
      • Only use stereo if your content actually benefits from it (this usually isn't the case; dialogue sounds better in mono)
    • Background streaming: on
  • Camera:
    • Video stabilization: Standard
    • Fixed horizon: off
    • Mirror front camera on stream: on
    • Color space: Standard RGB (streaming in HDR isn't supported by most streaming platforms, and will require additional configuration not mentioned here)
  • Audio:
    • Mic: [Select your recording microphone]
    • Bluetooth output only: on
    • Prefer stereo mic: off
  • Debug:
    • Builtin audio and video delay: 0

(when it comes to the encoder settings on your streaming server, just use whatever your target streaming platforms recommend)

don't forget to:

  • configure your overlays, scenes, and widgets as you see fit
  • configure all of the streaming platforms you stream to within Moblin (lets you read and moderate your chat within the app)
  • test everything end-to-end (so, do a test stream to your streaming server using all of your equipment) before going live!
