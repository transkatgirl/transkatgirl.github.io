AGI and AGI will not be separate events

🚧 this page is a work in progress!

in AI safety circles, there's a tendency to view AGI (Artificial General Intelligence) and ASI (Artificial Superintelligence) as two discreet events.

typically, this is how these terms are defined:

  • AGI = an AI system which matches or exceeds human capabilities on all cognitive tasks
  • ASI = an AI system with intelligence surpassing that of the most intelligent humans

however, if future AGI systems end up being architecturally similar to current LLMs, it's likely that our first AI system which reaches the threshold of AGI will also be superintelligent by most metrics.

today's LLMs are already superintelligent by some relatively narrow metrics, yet they still fail at certain cognitive tasks that even young children could complete with ease.

however, everything we've done to address the weaknesses of these systems hasn't made them worse at the tasks they already excel at. in fact, the opposite often seems to be the case: foundation models (but not task-specific fine tunes) improving in one benchmark tends to correlate with their performance improving in various unrelated benchmarks.

(however, there's one very notable exception to this: making LLMs into "assistants" has made them significantly worse at creative writing.
this has been known since GPT 3.5/ChatGPT, and there doesn't seem to be any other examples of such a large tradeoff resulting from attempting to improve overall LLM performance.)
